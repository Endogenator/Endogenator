# Why I Built This

Most mornings I open a conversation with an AI and work on something I don't yet understand well. The goal is to come out knowing more and able to do more than I did going in. If the thinking happens in the machine and stays there, I have gained nothing, so I build my working habits around keeping it.

*Section 1. The stance against offloading is stated and left unargued, as the brief asks.*

### Two leaks

The first leak is meaning that gets made and never taken up. An hour of work produces an insight, a cleaner framing, a decision about what to do next. Then the session closes, and the next day I am starting over or acting as if it never happened. Meaning that doesn't change what you do next hasn't done its work. I call this the uptake gap.

The second leak is quieter. AI output is the wording most likely to be believed. It arrives shaped like a conclusion, and a reader can take it up without ever seeing what it was a result of. It is like receiving the end result of an experiment without knowing the hypothesis. Two mistakes follow, and they will look familiar to anyone who has met Type I and Type II error. I can accept something that nothing supports, or I can discard something sound because I can't see why it works. Nothing in the prose helps me tell the two apart, since believable and grounded read the same on the page.

*Section 2, with the second facet folded in. The experiment line is the one memorable phrase. The Type I and Type II mapping is left in plain words so a general reader can follow it without the terms: first mistake is the false positive, second is the false negative. "Uptake gap" is the working label, pending your confirmation.*

### Why it happens to me

I am monotropic. Monotropism is a way of attending in which attention narrows into one interest at a time and goes very deep. Deep focus makes insight easy. The mundane work of carrying an insight forward, writing it down, connecting it to the last thing, coming back to it, is what falls away. That is a predictable pattern, and a predictable pattern can be designed around. I expect other people lose meaning the same way for other reasons: a full calendar, a noisy week, a tool that forgets.

*Section 3. Kept as a plain description of a pattern. No apology, no diagnosis story.*

### Tools as corrections

So I build tools that correct for it. The approach matters more than the list. I design around the way I actually work, and I treat each failure as information about what to build next. In practice that means shared anchor notes that hold the current state of each project, and update protocols that make carrying something forward a routine step and no longer a feat of memory. The anchors hold the reasoning along with the conclusion, which is the answer to the second leak. The AI I use most is Claude, and nothing here depends on that choice. The protocols are documented on the pages that follow, and the work behind them is on [my portfolio site](#).

### What I think AI is for

[Belief Space](/frameworks/belief-space) is the sum of what human minds have so far perceived or conceived. Each of us holds a region of it, and a region has edges, the places where what we can see runs out. I see three possible roles for AI at those edges, in ascending order.

The first is finding the edge. AI can show me where my understanding stops, which is hard to see from inside. The second is helping me cross it, by supplying what I need for the next step and then letting me test that step. The third is leaping to a belief space no one has occupied before. That is a possibility I am not yet claiming.

The uptake gap sits between the first two roles. An edge that is found and not carried forward is only a note. Crossing means that what AI shows me becomes something I act on and can check against the world, with its reasoning attached. Where things stand: edge-finding works. Crossing is partial. Leaping is an aspiration.
